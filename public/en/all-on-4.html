All-on-4 Concept

All-on-4 is a treatment concept in which a fixed (non-removable) prosthesis is planned on four implants in a jaw that has lost, or is about to lose, all its teeth. The two back implants are placed at an angle to use the denser bone regions, so that in many cases fixed teeth are possible without bone grafting or a sinus lift.

Performed by: Dt. Mehmet Taş & Dt. Aycan Işık Taş
  • Fixed prosthesis for the edentulous jaw
  • Graft-free planning in most cases
  • Temporary fixed prosthesis within days of surgery in suitable cases
  • Surgery: Dt. Mehmet Taş · Prosthesis: Dt. Aycan Işık Taş

In short: All-on-4 is a treatment concept that includes planning of a fixed prosthesis on 4 implants in case of total edentulism. Angled placement of implants aims to support the prosthesis. Suitability is determined by examination.

What is it?

In the classical approach a fixed prosthesis for an edentulous jaw needs 6–8 implants and often bone augmentation. In the All-on-4 concept the two front implants are placed upright and the two back implants at 30–45°; the angled implants stay in front of anatomical limits such as the sinus (upper jaw) and the nerve canal (lower jaw) and allow longer implants to be used. The four implants carry a single-piece prosthesis screwed onto them. Depending on bone volume and quality, a six-implant plan (All-on-6) may be chosen.

Who is suitable?

  • Patients who are edentulous or whose remaining teeth cannot be saved
  • Those who struggle with the retention and chewing of a removable full denture
  • Cases where bone loss would make classical implant planning dependent on grafting
  • Patients whose general health allows surgery (the criteria on the implant page apply)

Suitability is determined only with 3D tomography: no decision is made without measuring bone volume, density and anatomical limits. The condition of remaining teeth, the jaw relationship and lip support also affect the plan.

The process

  1. Assessment and digital planning: examination, CT, intra-oral and facial photographs; implant positions and prosthesis design are planned on the computer.
  2. Surgery: extractions if needed and placement of the four (or six) implants at the same visit. Under local anaesthesia, with sedation support if wished; usually 1.5–3 hours per jaw.
  3. Temporary fixed prosthesis: if the implants' initial stability is sufficient, a screw-retained temporary prosthesis is fitted within a few days. A soft diet and not overloading the implants are important during this period.
  4. Healing: 3–6 months. Gum adaptation and the prosthesis are monitored at check-ups.
  5. Final prosthesis: after impressions and try-ins, the final prosthesis (zirconia, titanium-framed ceramic or acrylic hybrid) is made and screwed in place. It can be removed by the dentist for cleaning, but not by the patient.

Who does what?

The surgical stage and implant planning are carried out by Dt. Mehmet Taş, the prosthesis design and prosthetic stages by Dt. Aycan Işık Taş. Digital planning is done by both dentists together; the shape of the prosthesis is decided before surgery.

Care

The underside of the prosthesis is cleaned daily with special floss (superfloss), interdental brushes and a water flosser. Once a year the prosthesis is removed to check the implants and screws. A night guard is advised for patients who clench or grind.

Visit plan for patients from abroad

First visit (7–10 days): examination, CT, surgery and temporary fixed prosthesis. Second visit (3–6 months later, 7–10 days): final prosthesis. A panoramic X-ray or CT is required for the remote preliminary assessment.

Points to consider

  • A few weeks of adaptation for eating and speaking are needed with the temporary prosthesis.
  • Peri-implant inflammation, fracture of the temporary prosthesis or screw loosening can occur; all can be dealt with at a check-up.
  • Smoking and poor hygiene markedly raise the risk of complications.
  • If check-ups are missed, bone loss around the implants can progress; yearly removal and cleaning of the prosthesis matters.
  • Extracting remaining teeth is irreversible; alternatives are weighed together at the examination.

Frequently asked questions

Is All-on-4 suitable for everyone?

No. Bone volume, density and anatomical limits are measured with CT; in some cases six implants, bone augmentation or a different type of prosthesis may be more appropriate.

Are four implants enough?

The concept distributes the load through angled placement and by connecting the implants to each other. If bone quality is low or chewing forces are high, six implants are planned; the decision is made with CT.

What can I eat with the temporary prosthesis?

A soft diet for the first 8–12 weeks; hard and sticky foods strain the implants while they fuse. After the final prosthesis, dietary restrictions largely end.

Can the prosthesis be removed?

Not by the patient; the dentist can unscrew it for check-ups and cleaning. This is the key difference from a removable denture.

How is the planning done?

Digital planning based on clinical examination, 3D tomography and photographs; the prosthesis design is decided before surgery.

How many visits are needed?

Usually two: 7–10 days for surgery and the temporary prosthesis, and 7–10 days for the final prosthesis 3–6 months later.

Is there a difference between upper and lower jaw?

Planning varies by jaw because bone structure is different.

What should be considered in the long term?

Regular control, oral hygiene and prosthesis maintenance are important.
