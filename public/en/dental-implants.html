Dental Implants

An implant is a titanium screw placed in the jawbone in place of a lost tooth root. Once it has fused with the bone, a crown, bridge or full denture is fitted on top. Because neighbouring teeth do not need to be ground down, it is a frequent choice for replacing missing teeth.

Performed by: Dt. Mehmet Taş
  • Single tooth, several teeth or a full jaw
  • Fusion with bone in 2–4 months; a temporary tooth on the same day in suitable cases
  • Planning with panoramic X-ray and CT where needed
  • Usually two visits for patients from abroad

In short: A dental implant is a titanium-based artificial root placed into the jawbone in case of missing teeth. Prosthetic planning is made on it for functional and esthetic rehabilitation. Suitability is determined by examination and radiological evaluation.

What is it?

A root-shaped titanium implant is placed in the jawbone under local anaesthesia. Titanium is biocompatible; within a few months the bone bonds to its surface (osseointegration). Once this has happened, a connector (abutment) and a crown are fitted. With several missing teeth, implants serve as bridge supports; in a fully edentulous jaw they carry a fixed or clip-retained denture.

Who is suitable?

Anyone whose growth is complete (generally over 18), who has enough jawbone and whose general health allows a surgical procedure may be a candidate. There is no upper age limit; general health is what matters. The following are assessed at the examination, and some can be corrected:

  • Uncontrolled diabetes: when controlled, results are close to those in healthy individuals; healing may take a little longer.
  • Smoking: markedly raises the risk of failed fusion and peri-implant inflammation; stopping ahead of surgery and during healing is advised.
  • Bisphosphonate / denosumab use: these osteoporosis and cancer medicines can impair jawbone healing; the decision is made together with the prescribing physician.
  • Radiotherapy to the head and neck, active gum disease, untreated infection: the relevant treatment comes first.
  • Blood thinners: not stopped; bleeding control is planned.

The process

  1. Assessment: examination, panoramic X-ray and, where needed, 3D tomography to measure bone volume, the nerve canal and the sinus; medical history and medicines are recorded.
  2. Surgical placement: under local anaesthesia, usually 30–60 minutes for a single implant. In suitable cases the implant is placed at the same visit as the extraction.
  3. Healing (osseointegration): 2–3 months in the lower jaw, 3–4 in the upper. A temporary tooth can be made for the front region during this time.
  4. Prosthesis: an impression (digital or conventional) is taken; the crown, bridge or implant-supported denture is made in the laboratory and fitted.
  5. Follow-up: every 3–6 months in the first year, then yearly check-ups and professional cleaning.

Not enough bone?

Bone shrinks in areas that have been toothless for a long time. This does not rule out implants; it changes the plan. Options include bone grafting (the patient's own bone or a graft material), sinus lift in the upper back region, short or narrow implants in suitable cases, and angled placement in the edentulous jaw (All-on-4 type planning). Grafting adds a few months to the treatment.

After the procedure

Cold compresses and the prescribed medicines are used on the first day; swelling and mild pain for 2–3 days are normal. Stitches are removed after 7–10 days. An implant cannot decay, but the gum and bone around it can become inflamed (peri-implantitis). Brushing twice a day, interdental brushes or floss and regular check-ups determine how long the implant lasts.

Visit plan for patients from abroad

Implant treatment usually needs two visits: the first for examination, CT and surgery (3–5 days); the second, 2–4 months later once the implant has fused, for impressions, try-in and fitting the prosthesis (5–8 days). A panoramic X-ray and photos are enough for the remote preliminary assessment; the final plan is confirmed at the examination in the clinic. See how it works for international patients.

Points to consider

  • Swelling, mild pain and slight oozing may occur after surgery; follow the care instructions given.
  • Smoking and poor oral hygiene raise the risk of peri-implant inflammation (peri-implantitis).
  • Uncontrolled systemic disease and some medicines affect healing; always mention them at the examination.
  • Bone loss around the implant, infection, screw loosening or prosthetic complications can occur; regular check-ups catch them early.
  • The long-term result depends on home care and dental check-ups.

Frequently asked questions

Is an examination necessary for implants?

Yes. Bone volume, the position of nerves and sinuses and general health are assessed by examination and imaging; a remote assessment only gives preliminary information.

Does the procedure hurt?

Placement is done under local anaesthesia; mild pain and swelling may follow for 2–3 days and are controlled with painkillers.

Can a tooth be fitted on the same day?

If bone quality and the implant's initial stability allow, a temporary tooth can be fitted the same day, especially in the front region. The permanent prosthesis is made after fusion with the bone is complete.

Can the body reject an implant?

Titanium is biocompatible and allergic rejection is very rare. Failure is usually due to infection, early loading or factors that impair healing, such as smoking.

What determines the treatment time?

Bone volume, the need for grafting, the number of implants and the type of prosthesis. A single implant and crown take 3–5 months in total; grafted cases may take longer.

Is a CT scan required?

Not in every case. Where bone volume is borderline or the site is close to a nerve or sinus, 3D imaging is needed for safe planning.

How long does an implant last?

It is not possible to give a single duration. It varies depending on maintenance, general health and regular controls.

Who may not be suitable for implants?

In individuals whose general health is not suitable and who have uncontrolled systemic disease, suitability is evaluated by the dentist.
