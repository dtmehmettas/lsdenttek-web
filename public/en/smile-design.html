Smile Design

Smile design is a planning process that aims to bring the colour, shape, size and alignment of the teeth into harmony with the lip line, gum level and facial features. The goal is not 'white and straight' but a smile that belongs to the person, is healthy and lasts. The result is seen before treatment starts, through digital design and a temporary trial in the mouth (mock-up).

Performed by: Dt. Aycan Işık Taş & Dt. Mehmet Taş
  • Planning with photographs, video and digital design
  • Preview with a temporary trial in the mouth (mock-up)
  • Priority to options that preserve tooth structure
  • Surgical coordination if gum contouring or implants are needed

In short: Smile design is a prosthetic and esthetic planning process where teeth, gingiva and facial esthetics are evaluated together. Personalized planning is made with examination.

What is it?

Smile design is not a single procedure but a planning method. On facial and intra-oral photographs, a smile video and a digital scan, the ideal proportions of the teeth are determined (width-to-height ratio, midline, relationship to the lip line, gum level); the target shape is designed on the computer and shown in the mouth with a temporary trial. This trial ensures the result matches expectations before treatment starts, and the design can still be changed at that stage.

Which methods are used?

Composite bonding: for small chips, gaps and colour corrections; composite material shaped directly on the tooth in a single visit. No or minimal preparation; shorter lifespan than porcelain, easy to repair.

Veneers (porcelain laminates): a 0.3–0.7 mm porcelain layer bonded to the front surface of the tooth. For colour, shape and mild alignment problems; very little tooth reduction, or none in suitable cases.

Zirconia / all-ceramic crowns: a restoration covering the whole tooth, for heavily filled, root-treated, fractured or severely discoloured teeth. See zirconia crowns.

Gum contouring: if the gum level is uneven or too much gum shows when smiling, the gum is reshaped to balance tooth length.

Whitening and orthodontics: whitening if colour is the only issue; for marked crowding and bite problems orthodontics comes before restorations. If teeth are missing, implant planning is coordinated with the surgical team.

Who is it for?

  • Discoloured teeth that do not respond to whitening
  • Chipped, worn, short or undersized teeth
  • Gaps between teeth (diastema) and mild crowding
  • Old fillings and crowns whose colour no longer matches
  • Uneven gum levels or a gummy smile

Gum disease and decay are treated before any aesthetic work; patients who clench or grind are given a night guard to protect the restorations.

The process

  1. Examination and records: teeth, gums and facial proportions are assessed; photographs, video, X-rays and a digital scan are taken.
  2. Digital design: the target shape is planned on the computer and reviewed together with the patient.
  3. Trial in the mouth (mock-up): the design is transferred onto the teeth in a temporary material; shape and length are decided at this stage.
  4. Preparation and impression: minimal tooth reduction if needed, digital impression, temporary restorations.
  5. Try-in and bonding: the restorations from the laboratory are tried for shade and fit; after approval they are bonded with an adhesive system. Bite and gum check after 1–2 weeks.

Care and lifespan

Porcelain restorations last many years with regular care; their lifespan depends on oral hygiene, clenching habits and the bite. Avoiding biting hard objects and keeping six-monthly check-ups is enough.

Visit plan for patients from abroad

Smile design with veneers and crowns is usually completed in a single visit of 7–10 days. For the preliminary assessment we ask for a smiling facial photo, intra-oral photos and a panoramic X-ray; the final plan is confirmed after the examination and digital design in the clinic.

Points to consider

  • Tooth preparation for veneers and crowns is irreversible; that is why no-prep options and the mock-up stage matter.
  • Aesthetic work does not last without healthy gums; gum treatment comes first where needed.
  • Expectations for colour and shape should match natural tooth structure and facial proportions; overly white, flat surfaces look unnatural.
  • For clenching and grinding habits a night guard is planned to protect the restorations.
  • Porcelain restorations can chip or need replacement over the years; regular check-ups are essential.

Frequently asked questions

What is smile design?

A personalised aesthetic planning process in which teeth and facial proportions are assessed together and the target shape is seen in advance through digital design and a temporary trial in the mouth.

Will my teeth be ground down?

Bonding usually needs no preparation. Veneers may need 0.3–0.7 mm of enamel preparation; some cases are done without any. Crowns need more preparation and are generally chosen for teeth that are already damaged.

Can I see the result beforehand?

Yes; with digital design and a temporary trial in the mouth (mock-up), shape and length are seen and can be changed before treatment starts.

How many visits?

Bonding is completed in a single visit. Veneers and crowns usually take 3–4 visits — examination/design, preparation and impression, bonding — over 7–10 days including laboratory time.

Can it be combined with whitening?

Yes; the order matters. Whitening comes first and the shade of the restorations is chosen 1–2 weeks later once the colour has settled; porcelain and composite do not whiten afterwards.

Which dentist does it?

Planning and prosthetic/aesthetic work by Dt. Aycan Işık Taş; if implants or gum surgery are needed for missing teeth, in coordination with Dt. Mehmet Taş.

Is smile design permanent?

It varies depending on the material used and maintenance; regular control is required.

Does it include gingival esthetics?

Gingival level can be evaluated as part of smile design.
