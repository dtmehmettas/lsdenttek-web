Zirconia Crowns

Zirconia is a white, metal-free ceramic framework material used in prosthetic dentistry. Because it transmits light it looks close to a natural tooth, it does not leave a dark line at the gum and it carries no risk of metal allergy. It is used for single crowns, bridges and implant-supported prostheses.

Performed by: Dt. Aycan Işık Taş
  • Metal-free; no grey line at the gum
  • Digital design and manufacture (CAD/CAM)
  • Usually 2–3 visits over 5–10 days
  • Monolithic (full zirconia) or porcelain-layered options

In short: Zirconia is a metal-free framework material used in prosthetic dentistry. It is evaluated for esthetic and durability purposes in crown and bridge prostheses.

What is it?

A crown is an artificial cap covering the whole of a tooth that has lost structure or has colour or shape problems. In a zirconia crown the framework is digitally milled from zirconium oxide ceramic; it is either layered with porcelain or made as a single piece of full zirconia (monolithic). In a metal-ceramic crown the framework is metal: it does not transmit light and a grey line may appear at the gum over the years.

When is it needed?

  • Teeth that have lost too much structure to be repaired with a filling, after large decay or fracture
  • Root-treated back teeth with an increased risk of fracture
  • Severe discolouration and shape problems where whitening and veneers are not enough
  • Bridges to replace a missing tooth; crowns and bridges on implants
  • Replacement of old crowns with poor margins or a visible metal line

A healthy tooth is not crowned for colour alone; in that case whitening or veneers, which remove less tooth structure, are considered first.

Front or back tooth?

In the front region aesthetics decide: multilayer zirconia with high light transmission, or veneers / all-ceramic crowns where enough tooth structure remains. In the back region durability comes first; full zirconia is very hard, reduces the risk of porcelain chipping and may be preferred for patients who clench or grind. The bite is adjusted carefully; a night guard extends the life of crowns.

The process

  1. Examination and X-ray: the health of the tooth, the need for root canal treatment and the condition of the gum are assessed; the shade is chosen in daylight against the neighbouring teeth.
  2. Tooth preparation: the tooth is reduced enough for the crown to fit; done under local anaesthesia.
  3. Impression: digital scan or conventional impression; a temporary crown is fitted.
  4. Laboratory: 3–7 days. The framework is milled digitally and layered with porcelain, or stained and glazed as a monolithic crown.
  5. Try-in and cementation: margins, bite and shade are checked, adjusted if needed and the crown is fixed. Check-up after 1–2 weeks.

Lifespan and care

Well-planned and well-maintained crowns generally last 10–15 years. What determines their lifespan is the health of the tooth underneath, gum care, clenching and regular check-ups. The crown itself cannot decay, but the tooth at its margin can; floss and interdental brushes therefore matter even more with crowned teeth.

Visit plan for patients from abroad

Zirconia crowns are usually completed in a single visit of 5–10 days: examination, preparation and impressions in the first days, then try-in and cementation after the laboratory stage. If root canal or gum treatment is needed, the stay may be longer.

Points to consider

  • Fit and seal at the crown margin are decisive; otherwise decay and gum inflammation can develop at the edge.
  • Biting hard objects or using teeth as tools can fracture a crown.
  • Clenching or grinding is taken into account in planning; a protective night guard may be needed.
  • Tooth preparation is irreversible; on a sound tooth, options that remove less structure are considered first.
  • Regular brushing, flossing and dental check-ups are required.

Frequently asked questions

Zirconia or metal-ceramic?

In the front region zirconia stands out for light transmission and gum compatibility; in the back region both give adequate durability. The decision depends on the tooth's position, remaining structure, the bite and the patient's preference, and is made at the examination.

Does a zirconia crown change colour?

The ceramic surface does not stain; no colour change is expected. If natural teeth are whitened the crown will not lighten, so whitening is done first.

Is it suitable for people with a metal allergy?

Yes; zirconia and all-ceramic options contain no metal.

What if there is pain under the crown?

Hot-cold sensitivity in the first weeks is temporary. Constant pain or pain on biting may mean the tooth under the crown needs root canal treatment; come in for a check.

Is an examination necessary?

Yes. Material choice, the amount of preparation and the need for root canal treatment can only be determined by examination and X-ray.

How many visits?

Usually 2–3: preparation and impression, try-in, cementation. With laboratory time, 5–10 days.

Does zirconia break?

As with any prosthetic material, fracture may be seen under excessive forces.

How is color selection made?

It is evaluated together with the dentist considering natural tooth color and patient expectation.
